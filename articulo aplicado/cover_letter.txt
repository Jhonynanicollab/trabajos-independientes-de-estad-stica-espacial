Dear Editor-in-Chief,

I am pleased to submit my original research article entitled "Spatio-Temporal
Cluster Detection of Forest Fires in Southern Peru (2017-2025) Using the
SaTScan Method and Spatial Autocorrelation" for consideration for publication
in Natural Hazards.

This study integrates 74,861 satellite-derived fire hotspots (NASA FIRMS
VIIRS/MODIS) and 2,727,647 deforestation alerts (GeoBosques-MINAM) across
237 districts of southern Peru (Madre de Dios, Puno, and Cusco) to detect
statistically significant spatio-temporal fire clusters using SaTScan's
discrete Poisson model, complemented by global and local spatial
autocorrelation analysis (Moran's Index and LISA). We identify nine
significant clusters, reveal extreme seasonal concentration of fire
occurrence linked to drought and El Nino episodes, and demonstrate a strong
spatial association between prior deforestation and fire risk in
neighboring districts.

We believe this work aligns closely with the scope of Natural Hazards,
particularly its focus on climatological hazards, risk assessment, and
early-warning systems, and provides empirical evidence directly relevant to
fire-prevention policy in the Peruvian Amazon and Andes.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not
under consideration elsewhere. The author declares no competing interests
and no external funding was received for this research.
